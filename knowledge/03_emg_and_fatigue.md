# Surface EMG during fatiguing exercise

During sustained or repeated sub-maximal contractions, surface EMG amplitude usually rises as the muscle fatigues, because additional motor units are recruited and firing patterns change to keep the force output up. The spectrum also shifts toward lower frequencies, which is a classic fatigue signature described in the EMG literature (for example De Luca, 1997).

A rising amplitude with slowing tempo is therefore consistent with accumulating fatigue. Near true exhaustion, amplitude can plateau or fall when the person can no longer drive the muscle, so a drop in amplitude together with stalled reps should not be read as recovery.

Limits of this project's sensor: the Advancer Muscle Sensor v3 outputs a rectified and smoothed envelope, not the raw signal, so spectral fatigue measures cannot be computed. Amplitude depends on electrode placement, skin impedance, sweat and gain, so values should be compared within one session and normalised to the first few reps of the set, not across days or people.

Use of the amplitude trend: report it as a percentage change versus the first three reps. Combine it with tempo loss; neither signal alone is reliable.

General summary for a student project, not medical advice.
