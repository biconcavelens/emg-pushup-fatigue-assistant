# Velocity loss and tempo loss as a stopping rule

Velocity-based training uses the drop in rep velocity within a set as an objective fatigue measure. Sanchez-Medina and Gonzalez-Badillo (2011) reported that the percentage loss of velocity during a set relates to metabolic fatigue markers, so velocity loss is a practical indicator of how close to failure a set is.

Pareja-Blanco et al. (2017) compared stopping sets at 20 % velocity loss with 40 % velocity loss in resistance training. The 20 % group did fewer reps per set and accumulated less fatigue, yet gained strength and muscle size comparable to the 40 % group, with some measures favouring the lower-fatigue approach. Commonly used velocity-loss bands are roughly 10 to 20 % for keeping quality high, 20 to 30 % for a hard but controlled set, and 40 % or more for sets close to failure.

For body-weight push-ups at a fixed range of motion, rep duration is a proxy for velocity: a longer rep is a slower rep. Tempo loss can be computed as 1 minus (reference rep time divided by current rep time), using the median of the first reps as the reference.

Suggested bands for this assistant: below 10 % fresh, 10 to 20 % building fatigue, 20 to 35 % stop zone (recommend ending the set), 35 % or more at or near failure.
