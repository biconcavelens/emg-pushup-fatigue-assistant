# Push-up technique and form cues

Main muscles: chest (pectoralis major), front of the shoulder (anterior deltoid), and the triceps at the back of the upper arm; the trunk and glutes hold the body in a straight line.

Cues: hands about shoulder width or slightly wider, elbows roughly 30 to 45 degrees from the torso instead of flared out, body in a straight line from head to heels, lower until the chest is near the floor, press up to full arm extension without shrugging.

A controlled tempo of about 2 seconds down and 1 second up keeps the load on the muscles instead of bouncing. Rep times that stretch well beyond the starting tempo indicate fatigue.

Form breakdown signs: hips sagging or rising, shortened range of motion, head dropping forward, elbows flaring. When these appear, end the set; continuing with poor form shifts load to joints and does not add useful training.

Regressions when reps are too hard: incline push-ups with hands on a bench, or knee push-ups. Progressions when sets are easy: slower tempo, pauses at the bottom, feet elevated.

General summary for a student project, not medical advice.
