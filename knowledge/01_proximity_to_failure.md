# Proximity to failure and reps in reserve (RIR)

Reps in reserve (RIR) is the number of repetitions a person could still complete with acceptable form when a set ends. An RIR-based effort scale was described by Zourdos et al. (2016) for resistance training; RIR 0 means the set ended at failure, RIR 2 means two more reps were possible.

Training to failure is not required for most goals. Sets ended at roughly 1 to 3 reps in reserve usually give most of the training stimulus while leaving the athlete less fatigued, so later sets and later sessions keep their quality. Failure sets cost more recovery time, and the rep count of the next set typically drops more.

For push-ups, approaching failure shows up as slower reps, longer pauses at the bottom, a grinding last rep, and loss of body alignment such as sagging hips. Those are signs to end the set with form intact instead of pushing past them.

Practical rule for an EMG assistant: end the set when tempo loss reaches the stop zone, and treat "grinding" reps with rising muscle activation as a sign of approaching failure.

This is a general summary for a student project, not medical advice.
